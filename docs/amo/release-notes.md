0.7.0 was the last version on this listing; 0.8.0 to 0.13.0 were released on GitHub only. What changed since 0.7.0:

- 0.14.0: **Particles count as known.** The word colours show particles, and their combinations with the copula and the auxiliaries (は, には, から, です, ですね, という …), in green wherever they stand, as grammar you know rather than words with a card; a new switch in the popup, "Particles count as known", on by default, turns it off. A particle still never takes the colour of the word before it. The browser's word splitter cuts a verb it does not know into pieces shaped like particles (やって, 飲んだ, 書かない), and those stay uncoloured: measured on real subtitles, about one green particle in thirty is still such a piece. **Names and Latin text are blue**, no card needed: place names (東京, 丸の内, アメリカ, and 東京駅 or 渋谷区 with their suffix) and Latin words (jr, YouTube). A longer name wins over a shorter card, so 東京駅 is blue even with 東京 and 駅 in your deck. **Your own known words.** A list in the popup, one word per line, colours those words green whatever their card says and finds them in their conjugations without a card; **Alt+Shift+K** puts the word under the pointer (or the one Yomitan has selected) on the list, or takes it off again. "Katakana words count as known" is a switch of its own, off by default. None of these needs a deck: with nothing mined yet, or Anki closed, they are coloured all the same. A card for a noun also colours the する forms after it (お願いします, 勉強している), and the pitch accent overbar reads Yomitan's drawn graph (`{pitch-accent-graphs}`) too, while a pitch field that holds nothing but the reading no longer counts as heiban.
- 0.13.0: **A word you mined in kanji is coloured when the subtitle writes it in kana.** A card for 更に now colours さらに, for words your dictionary marks as usually written in kana (Jitendex and JMdict both say so); other kanji words are not matched by their reading, since that painted 勝手 inside 向かって. **A long line is never cut inside a word.** When someone talks for ten seconds without a pause, the line has to be split somewhere, and it used to land in the middle of 学校 or 能力; it now breaks between words. A video you watched before is transcribed again on your next visit, automatically.
- 0.12.0: **Lines that are sentences.** Whisper punctuates spoken Japanese when it feels like it: the same audio decoded twice gave そうなんですよね。 once and nothing the next time, and a line the server cannot see the end of runs on into the next speaker's. Shisu-ko now asks for the punctuation, with a short Japanese sentence given to Whisper before every stretch of audio, and where it still writes none, a sentence-final expression followed by a pause gets its mark. Two sentences then never share a row: the second gets a row of its own, or a line of its own. On a conversational video, line breaks that fall where a sentence ends went from about two thirds to nine in ten, and lines longer than 25 characters from a fifth to under a tenth, so a line reads as one thought again, and the card you mine from it holds one sentence and not the rest of the conversation. **Whole lines are no longer deleted as noise.** A quality check measured how long each word lasts, and Japanese kana are shorter than it expected, so ordinary speech scored as gibberish: in fifteen minutes of one video it threw away five real utterances, one of them ten seconds long. It now judges a line on how likely its words are, not on how fast they are spoken. **A video you watched before gets the new lines automatically** on your next visit: the saved transcript of an older release is ignored and the video transcribed again, and nothing needs deleting. **Word colours mark the word and nothing else:** a particle after a coloured word has no card of its own, so it stays plain (領域まで shows 領域 in colour and まで in black). The honorific お or ご still joins the word it fronts, and a word written in kana is still found in its conjugations.
- 0.11.3: **Sung lyrics.** Singing is not speech to the voice-activity detector, so a music video or a 歌枠 used to stay blank. A window in which the detector hears next to nothing but the audio is not silent is now transcribed without it, once Whisper hears Japanese in it and under stricter checks on Whisper's own confidence, so songs get their lyrics; a song in another language, rain, a crowd or an engine stay blank. A video watched before this release gets its lines on the next visit without touching the cache; `--lyrics off` on the server restores the old behaviour. The status badge says "No speech found in this video" when the whole video was transcribed and nothing was heard, instead of leaving you to wonder. Word colours read a line in whole pieces: the honorific お or ご joins the word it fronts (お風呂), a word is found after the quotative って or と (っていう), and a word written in kana is found in its forms too (かける colours かけて, おいしい colours おいしかった).
- **Subtitle lines that read like subtitles.** A line is never cut inside a word: Japanese writes no spaces, and speakers pause mid-word, so splitting on a pause alone used to break 言ってた across two lines. The splitter now reads the change of script and the usual line-breaking rules instead. Neighbouring lines merge across Whisper's own segment boundaries, which they never did before: on a fast conversation the text used to change 27 times a minute with a median line of seven characters. A line holds a beat longer after the sound stops, so it does not vanish on its last syllable, and it may run to seven seconds, the length Netflix allows itself. And the first word of each passage of speech, which Whisper timestamps seconds too early, is repaired before the quality checks judge it, so whole lines are no longer thrown away as noise: 29 lost lines in seventeen minutes became 8.
- **A mined card gets the line that was on screen**, not the stretch of speech around it, so the sentence on the card and its audio are what you read and heard; a line the server broke in two keeps that break on the card. The left arrow key steps back a whole line instead of replaying the one already playing.
- **Word colours.** Two options in the popup, both off by default. "Colour words by their Anki card" colours every word of a subtitle, and of the transcript, that has a card in your deck by the card's state: green learned, yellow learning, orange suspended, red new; a verb or adjective is found in its conjugations. "Overbar by pitch accent" draws a bar over each such word in the colour of its pitch accent pattern (blue heiban, red atamadaka, orange nakadaka, green odaka), read from the card's pitch accent field as Yomitan writes it. The deck is the one your last mined card went to, or the one you choose; it is read again every half minute while a video is open, and the text stays scannable by Yomitan. Needs Anki with AnkiConnect, like mining.
- Setup asks which Whisper model to use, large-v3 or small, and downloads it with a progress bar; the server starts on that model unless told otherwise.
- A new card is matched to its subtitle by sentence and word (the note's word field, or the one named in the popup), and the screenshot and audio of every sentence are prepared while it plays, so the card is filled the moment it appears.
- The transcript panel shortcut is Alt+Shift+L (Chrome reserves Alt+Shift+T for its toolbar).
- The same source also runs in Chrome; the Chrome package is attached to the GitHub release.
- The Whisper model is picked in the popup: any faster-whisper size or Hugging Face repo id, downloaded on first use and swapped in while the video plays, without restarting the server.
- A subtitle font can be any font installed on the computer; the popup previews it and says whether the name was found.
- 0.9.0: **updates from the popup.** The extension looks at GitHub for the newest release (once a day, again at the next opening after a failed check, or on "Check for updates" in the popup) and, when the server you are running is older, shows a toolbar badge, one notification per browser session and a banner with an **Update** button: the server exits, its launcher installs the update and starts it again, and the popup follows the restart. A server not started by run.cmd / run.sh (Docker, Nix, by hand) is told to restart by hand instead. The extension itself is updated by Firefox from this listing; the new **notifications** permission ("Display notifications to you") is for those notifications, and Firefox asks you to approve it before it installs this update over an older version.
- 0.8.0: a **Start server** button in the popup. While the server is offline, one click starts it, with the server's default options, through a small launcher that the server's setup registers with Firefox (every start of the server registers it too, from the first start after the update to 0.8.0); Firefox asks once for permission to exchange messages with it.
